# Proposed Curriculum Redesign
## B.Tech CSE (Artificial Intelligence and Data Science)

## 1. Overview

This proposal aims to improve the alignment of the B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence and Data Science) curriculum with current industry requirements.

The redesign focuses on strengthening practical learning, updating selected technical topics, reducing demonstrable duplication and introducing industry-oriented activities. It aims to retain essential foundations while providing students with opportunities to apply their knowledge to realistic problems.

The proposal is intended for academic consideration and does not represent an approved change to the university curriculum.

## 2. Redesign Objectives

The proposed redesign has five objectives:

1. Retain essential programming, mathematical, database and computing foundations.
2. Strengthen practical work in data analytics and machine learning.
3. Introduce modern AI application development and evaluation practices.
4. Reduce unnecessary repetition where detailed syllabus comparison supports it.
5. Maintain balanced programme credits and preserve prerequisites and required learning outcomes.

## 4. Proposed Learning Model

The redesign recommends combining theoretical instruction with practical work throughout the programme.

### Stage 1: Foundations

Students develop programming, mathematics, database and core computing knowledge. Assessments should test conceptual understanding and practical problem-solving.

### Stage 2: Applied data and AI

Students use realistic datasets to perform data preparation, analysis, visualisation and machine-learning tasks. They should explain their methods and justify their conclusions.

### Stage 3: Modern AI applications

Students develop small applications using suitable AI models and APIs. They learn to test outputs, evaluate reliability, document limitations and handle data responsibly.

### Stage 4: Deployment and integrated projects

Students combine programming, databases, analysis, AI and software engineering in an end-to-end project. The project includes version control, testing, deployment where appropriate, and a final report.

This staged model builds on earlier learning rather than treating each course as an isolated subject.

## 5. Proposed Credit-Reallocation Strategy

The assignment requires proposed changes affecting approximately 20–25% of total programme credits. The redesign should demonstrate this through a transparent calculation.

Let:

- \(T\) = total verified programme credits.
- \(C\) = credits affected by the proposed redesign.

The target is:

\[
0.20T \leq C \leq 0.25T
\]

The affected credits should represent identifiable course components or courses whose learning outcomes, content, practical work or allocation are proposed to change.

### Illustrative calculation

If a verified programme contained 160 credits, the target range would be:

\[
32 \leq C \leq 40
\]

This is an example only, not the confirmed total for this programme.

### Credit-planning principles

1. Count only verified programme credits.
2. Identify exactly which courses or course components are affected.
3. Distinguish content updates from actual credit changes.
4. Do not count the same credits twice across multiple recommendations.
5. Preserve prerequisites, required learning outcomes and applicable academic requirements.
6. Keep the overall programme credit total balanced unless the official regulations explicitly permit a change.

The final calculation will be completed after the complete semester-wise curriculum and internship-credit requirements have been established.

## 7. Evaluation Measures

The redesigned learning approach can be evaluated using:

- Completion of end-to-end projects.
- Accuracy and quality of data preparation.
- Appropriate selection and interpretation of model evaluation measures.
- Reliability testing of AI-generated outputs.
- Quality of documentation and reproducibility.
- Successful completion of practical deployment tasks.
- Ability to explain assumptions, limitations and design decisions.

These are proposed evaluation measures. No improvement in student performance is claimed without actual evaluation data.

## 8. Risks and Safeguards

### Risk 1: Reducing important foundations

**Safeguard:** Review prerequisites and programme learning outcomes before reducing any topic or credit.

### Risk 2: Introducing tools without sufficient fundamentals

**Safeguard:** Introduce modern AI tools after students understand the relevant programming, data and machine-learning concepts.

### Risk 3: Overloading existing courses

**Safeguard:** Compare course workload and consolidate duplication before adding substantial new practical work.

### Risk 4: Dependence on rapidly changing technologies

**Safeguard:** Teach transferable principles such as testing, evaluation, data quality and responsible AI alongside specific tools.

### Risk 5: Unbalanced credit allocation

**Safeguard:** Calculate affected credits, preserve required programme totals and obtain the necessary academic approvals.

## 9. Expected Benefits

The proposed redesign is expected to provide a clearer connection between academic learning and workplace tasks. It prioritises practical implementation, reliable model evaluation, modern AI application development, collaboration and responsible use of technology.

The proposal also provides a structured way to identify redundant content without assuming that foundational or non-specialised subjects lack value.

## 10. Conclusion

The proposed redesign retains essential foundations while strengthening practical learning and updating selected AI and data-related topics. It recommends reducing content only when duplication or limited learning value can be demonstrated and introducing new learning activities where evidence identifies gaps.

The final implementation plan will use verified programme credits, detailed syllabus comparisons, industry evidence, prerequisite checks and applicable academic requirements to ensure that the recommendations are transparent, balanced and feasible.
