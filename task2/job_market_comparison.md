Job-Market Analysis and Curriculum Comparison

## 1. Objective

The objective of this analysis is to identify the technical skills employers currently seek and compare them with the B.Tech Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence and Data Science) curriculum.

The analysis focuses on three areas:

- Skills already supported by existing courses.
- Industry requirements that may need stronger practical coverage.
- New topics that could improve students' readiness for AI, machine learning, and data-related roles.

## 5. Main Skill Gaps to Investigate

### 5.1 From theory to practical implementation

Knowing machine-learning concepts is important, but the reviewed roles also involve building, testing and validating working solutions. Courses should give students opportunities to complete practical projects from data preparation through evaluation.

### 5.2 Generative AI application development

The Luxoft posting specifically mentions retrieval-augmented generation, embeddings, AI agents and LLM evaluation. A Generative AI course can cover these topics through a small application that retrieves information from trusted documents and evaluates answer quality.

### 5.3 Data pipelines and deployment

Employers need solutions that can work beyond a classroom demonstration. Practical assignments should expose students to data pipelines, version control, APIs, deployment and basic monitoring.

### 5.4 Reproducibility and collaboration

Students should practise using Git, documenting decisions, explaining data sources, recording limitations and collaborating on projects. These habits make technical work easier to review and maintain.

### 5.5 Responsible AI and communication

Students should learn to explain model limitations, check outputs, protect sensitive information and communicate findings clearly. These skills support reliable use of AI in real working environments.

## 6. Recommended Direction

Based on the reviewed postings, the curriculum should retain its core programming, database, data science and machine-learning foundations while strengthening practical implementation.

The main improvement areas are:

1. More realistic, end-to-end projects.
2. Stronger model evaluation and validation.
3. Applied Generative AI and RAG exercises.
4. Data pipelines, APIs and deployment practice.
5. Git-based collaboration and technical documentation.
6. Responsible AI, testing and clear communication.

## 7. Limitations

The analysis covers three job postings from two employers, including roles with different experience expectations. The findings identify useful industry signals but do not prove that every employer requires every listed skill.

Course titles alone do not establish the depth of practical coverage. Final recommendations should therefore consider the detailed course syllabi, existing laboratory work, prerequisites, programme-credit limits and applicable academic regulations.

## 8. Conclusion

The comparison indicates that the curriculum contains several subjects aligned with AI and data-related work. The opportunity for improvement is to connect these subjects more closely with practical engineering tasks, modern AI applications, data workflows, model evaluation and deployment.

The next stage of the audit will translate these findings into specific KEEP, UPDATE, REDUCE and ADD recommendations, supported by course-level evidence and a balanced credit plan.
